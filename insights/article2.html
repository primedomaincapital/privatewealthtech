RIA Tech Stack: What Top Firms Actually Run On

Orion, Tamarac, Black Diamond, Redtail: what the fastest-growing RIAs actually run, layer by layer, and the technology stack decisions that compound in 2026.

By Prime Domain Capital · October 2026

Monday morning at a $2 billion RIA. The COO has four browser tabs open, three CSV exports downloading, and a reconciliation that doesn’t. The firm spent six figures on software last year. None of it talks to the rest.

The modern RIA doesn’t have a technology problem. It has an integration problem. According to Schwab’s 2025 benchmarking data, 89% of top-performing RIAs treat technology as a strategic advantage rather than an operational necessity. The difference between those firms and everyone else is not how much they spend — it is how the pieces fit. Advisors don’t adopt software. They adopt whatever removes the next manual step.

The four layers

Portfolio management and trading is the operational core — performance reporting, aggregation, rebalancing, model management, trade execution. This is where investment decisions become transactions, and the vendor choices here are the most consequential a firm makes.

Orion is the industry standard for independent RIAs: portfolio accounting, trading and rebalancing through its Eclipse engine, reporting, billing, and client portals in one platform. According to X1 Wealth’s 2026 advisor platforms comparison, 17 of the top 20 Barron’s-ranked RIA firms run on Orion technology. In February 2026, Orion launched Denali AI Enterprise, pulling CRM, planning, portfolio management, and risk tools into a single connected AI view — the clearest signal yet that the all-in-one platforms intend to absorb the AI layer rather than cede it.

Envestnet Tamarac is the choice for multi-advisor teams that want rebalancing, CRM, and reporting in a single system, with deep ties to the broader Envestnet ecosystem. Envestnet has raised its Tamarac technology investment to 2.5 times its prior level — including a $35 million commitment — as part of a five-year, $1 billion R&D program. Its 2026 releases added Report Studio for RIAs, a customizable wealth management dashboard, and AI-assisted report migration now in beta, with a natural-language account-data agent following.

SS&C Black Diamond serves mid-to-large firms where portfolio analytics and accounting depth are the priority, supporting over 3,000 firms with Morningstar research embedded. Addepar owns the alternatives-heavy and institutional end — multi-asset-class accounting with the customization that family offices and large RIAs demand. Morningstar Advisor Workstation and eMoney fill the research-led and planning-integrated niches.

The modern RIA doesn’t have a technology problem. It has an integration problem.

The CRM layer is where adoption is won or lost. Redtail holds broader market share with deeper integrations across legacy advisor tools. Wealthbox, with its cleaner interface, is the preferred choice for tech-forward firms onboarding staff new to advisor-specific CRM. Roughly 80% of RIAs run one of them. The choice usually comes down to which one the team will actually use — the best CRM is the one with complete data in it.

Underpinning all four layers is data aggregation — the unglamorous plumbing that pulls positions and transactions from custodians every night. When aggregation breaks, every downstream number is suspect: performance reports misstate, billing miscalculates, and the CRM shows stale balances to the advisor walking into a client meeting. Firms that treat aggregation as infrastructure, monitored daily rather than repaired quarterly, report materially fewer reconciliation fires — and their advisors trust the numbers on screen.

Financial planning — eMoney, MoneyGuidePro, RightCapital — has moved from differentiator to table stakes. What matters now is how tightly planning data flows into the portfolio and CRM layers. Orion’s Denali, Tamarac’s MoneyGuide integration, and Black Diamond’s planning connectors all reflect the same insight: a plan that doesn’t update when the portfolio moves is a PDF, not a system.

The underinvested layers are where growth is being left on the table. Transition automation — the technology for onboarding advisors and their clients — sits at roughly 15% adoption. AI-powered compliance workflow automation is near 25%. CRM and portfolio management are near-universal; the tools that actually enable growth are adopted by fewer than a quarter of practices. Fidelity Clearing & Custody projects that AI and automation tools could boost wealth manager productivity by 25 to 40% — for a three-advisor team, a 25% gain is a full-time operations hire without the headcount.

The build-vs-buy question

No platform wins on every dimension. Orion leads on breadth for domestic mid-market RIAs; Addepar on multi-asset complexity; Black Diamond on accounting depth; Tamarac on the integrated advisor desktop. FactSet and BlackRock’s Aladdin Wealth serve the institutional end where analytics depth justifies enterprise implementation cost.

The decision framework that works has five variables: client complexity, the compliance environment, product architecture, the team’s actual adoption capacity, and integration depth with the custodian. Schwab, Fidelity, and Pershing integrations are not a footnote — reconciliation breaks, data lags, and manual workarounds live or die on custodial connectivity.

The pattern among the fastest-growing firms is consistent: integrated stacks over best-of-breed point solutions, chosen for the integrations that eliminate the most manual steps first. Start with the reconciliation that eats Monday morning. Everything else is commentary.
